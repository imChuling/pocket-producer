# Protocol: grouping threshold for the fusion score (v1)

Written 2026-11-17, before any score in this protocol was computed. A local
freeze in the repository, not an independently timestamped preregistration.

## Question

The product groups a new fragment with earlier ones. Can the deployed
`fusion-5sig-v1` score between two loops make the join / do not join call
against one fixed threshold, and is it better at it than plain MS-CLAP
cosine? The weights were trained on pack co-membership (two loops filed in
the same pack), which is the same question grouping asks, but on pack data,
not on anyone's own grouping choices.

## Data

- Items: `research/data/fsld/items_msclap_packonly.jsonl` (pack-only FSLD
  items with MS-CLAP embeddings; gitignored, regenerated by the pipeline in
  `research/README.md`).
- Split: the frozen pack split `artifacts/heldout-split-packonly/split.json`
  (484 train packs, 121 held-out packs, seed 20260810). Not changed here.
- Uploaders: from the FSLD manifest, as in
  `research/run_uploader_overlap_check.py`. Only counts are written out.

## Pairs

Every unordered pair of items on the same side of the split. A pair is
positive when both items are in the same pack, negative otherwise. Pairs
never cross the split.

## Scores

1. Fusion: the deployed weights `artifacts/fusion-deploy/weights.json`
   applied to `[cos, cos, tempo, key, tag_jaccard]` for the two items, with
   no bias. With one loop on each side the mean and max cosine terms are the
   same cosine. Tempo is `tempo_match` taken in both directions and averaged
   (it is asymmetric, and a pair has no natural direction). Key is
   `key_match` in exact mode after `serving_key`. Tag Jaccard is over
   lower-cased tag sets. This is the function the product will call
   (`backend/ranking/grouping.py`); the script checks its vectorised scores
   against that function on a sample of pairs.
2. Baseline: MS-CLAP cosine alone.

## Threshold rule (chosen on train packs only)

For each score, the threshold is the smallest value at which pairwise
precision on train-side pairs reaches 0.50, so that a "join" is right at
least as often as it is wrong. If no threshold reaches 0.50, the rule falls
back to the threshold that maximises F1 on train-side pairs, and the report
says so. The F1-maximising threshold is also reported for both scores as a
secondary reference.

Caveat stated in advance: the fusion weights were fitted on these train
packs, so train-side fusion scores are in-sample and the threshold may be
chosen on an optimistic distribution. The held-out numbers carry that.

## Held-out report (computed once)

On held-out pairs, at each score's own primary threshold:

- precision and recall, with 95% percentile intervals from a pack-level
  bootstrap (10,000 resamples of the 121 packs with replacement; positives
  are pairs within a resampled pack, negatives are pairs between two
  distinct resampled packs; seed 20261117);
- average precision for both scores, and the fusion minus cosine difference
  in average precision with its bootstrap interval.

Fusion is called better than cosine for this task only if that average
precision difference has an interval entirely above zero. If it does not,
the product change that would rely on it is not made until this is
reported.

The same numbers are reported for the uploader-disjoint subset: held-out
packs whose uploader has no pack on the train side, with pairs among those
packs only. This subset is descriptive.

## Known gaps, stated in advance

- Pack co-membership is a proxy for "belongs in the same project". No user
  grouping data is used anywhere, and none exists to check against.
- Online tags come from the product's tagging step, not FSLD annotations,
  and online tempo and key come from analysis, not pack metadata. The
  distributions of the tempo, key and tag terms online can differ from
  these, so the threshold may not transfer exactly.
- Pairwise precision here is measured against every different-pack pair in
  a library of a few hundred loops from many people. A single user's library
  has a different mix of related and unrelated loops.
