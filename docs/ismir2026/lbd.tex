\begin{abstract}
Musicians accumulate large personal libraries of unfinished audio fragments,
yet re-entering an unfinished project offers little support for deciding which
fragment to try next.
We formulate \emph{session-conditioned continuation retrieval}: given the live
state of a digital audio workstation (DAW) project, rank a musician's own
fragments by their utility for the next creative step, rather than by generic
acoustic similarity.
We present Pocket Producer, a prototype that reads a live Audiotool project
through the Nexus API, derives a transparent session fingerprint, and ranks
personal fragments using an explainable rule baseline and frozen audio--text
representations (MS-CLAP 2023).
Every recommendation is previewable, grounded in structured evidence, and
insertable into the project timeline through a single reversible transaction.
On 1,755 weak pairs from the Freesound Loop Dataset, frozen CLAP cosine
similarity already achieves 0.883 pairwise accuracy, and learned rerankers
(up to 335K parameters) cannot exceed this baseline.
However, a five-parameter logistic fusion of audio cosine, tempo match, key
match, and tag overlap reaches 0.794 on the hardest negatives
(embedding-similar cross-source items), a +4.0\,pp gain over cosine alone
(0.754; sign test $p = 0.031$), demonstrating that music-theory signals
complement audio embeddings even under weak supervision.
Session-structure signals (instrument role gap, harmonic compatibility)
show no gain on pack-membership labels, highlighting the need for
task-specific evaluation.
An external evaluation (9~raters, 20~pairs, Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.294$)
shows that continuation preference is subjective but far from random,
motivating evaluation protocols beyond acoustic similarity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Loop-based music production accumulates a growing library of short audio
fragments---recorded ideas, downloaded samples, half-finished stems.
When a musician returns to an unfinished project, the central question is not
``what sounds similar?''\ but ``what would be useful as the next step?''
Existing work on loop compatibility~\cite{chen2020neuralloop,lattner2022samplematch,yi2024drum2bass}
and stem coherence~\cite{ciranni2024cocola,nistal2024diffariff}
addresses whether two audio elements can co-exist in a mix, but the query in
our setting is a \emph{live session state}, not a single reference loop, and
the candidates are the musician's own creative memory, not a public catalog.

We define \textbf{continuation utility} as the property that a candidate
fragment would help the musician make progress in the current session---a
concept that subsumes tempo fit, timbral contrast, structural gap-filling, and
the musician's stated intent, but cannot be reduced to any single acoustic
similarity metric.
This formulation yields three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Task:} We cast personal fragment retrieval as a
  session-conditioned ranking problem with a preregistered primary metric
  (pairwise accuracy over human continuation preferences).
\item \textbf{System:} We implement a working prototype in a real DAW
  (Audiotool) with live document reading, reversible insertion, and
  explainable evidence for each recommendation.
\item \textbf{Protocol:} We report a backbone bake-off, a baseline comparison
  showing that learned rerankers cannot beat frozen cosine but
  multi-signal fusion can (+4.0\,pp on hard negatives), and a blind
  A/B evaluation with 9~external raters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Session Fingerprint}\label{sec:fingerprint}

When a project is opened, the system queries the Nexus document for
\texttt{audioTrack}, \texttt{noteTrack}, \texttt{patternTrack}, and
\texttt{automationTrack} entities, and reads the tempo from the project
configuration entity.
These are mapped deterministically to a \texttt{SessionFingerprint}:
tempo (or \texttt{null} if absent), track count and classified track types,
playhead position, and a user-editable text intent field.
Only this summary leaves the browser; the raw Audiotool document is never
sent to the Pocket Producer backend.
Fields the document does not provide are reported as missing, never imputed.
When session audio regions are available, their samples are downloaded and
encoded with MS-CLAP 2023~\cite{elizalde2023clap} to form a mean session
audio embedding, enabling the \texttt{mean-session-v1} baseline.
We chose CLAP over recent self-supervised alternatives such as
MERT~\cite{li2024mert} and MuQ~\cite{zhu2025muq} because
CLAP's joint audio-text space supports the text-intent retrieval path;
a systematic comparison of pretrained audio representations for
recommendation is given by Tamm and Aljanaki~\cite{tamm2026representations}.
Additionally, a linear probe trained on the bake-off's 6-class instrument
role labels (Section~\ref{sec:bakeoff}) predicts the role of each region
and candidate from their CLAP embeddings, and CQT-based chroma vectors
(12-dim, mean-aggregated) are extracted for harmonic analysis.

\subsection{Continuation Utility Ranking}\label{sec:ranking}

Candidate fragments are scored in two stages.
First, per-space retrieval runs independently over four signal spaces:
(1)~structured metadata (tempo/key/tag matching),
(2)~frozen CLAP embeddings (cosine similarity),
(3)~\emph{role-gap fill}---a zero-shot instrument probe identifies which
roles (e.g.\ bass, drums, melody) are present in the session and boosts
candidates whose predicted role fills a gap, a compositional signal that
cosine similarity over mixed-down audio fundamentally cannot capture, and
(4)~\emph{harmonic compatibility}---transpose-invariant cross-correlation
of CQT chroma vectors scores tonal fit across all 12 semitone shifts and
reports the best transposition (validated on 168 synthetic trials with
100\% alignment recovery; on 500 FSLD same-pack pairs, harmonic score
predicts pack membership at 65.2\%, above the 50\% chance baseline).
Results are fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion~\cite{cormack2009rrf}.
Second, a capacity ladder of rerankers is applied.
Table~\ref{tab:capacity} shows the three tiers, all within a hard budget
of 500K trainable parameters and running on CPU.

The \texttt{rules-v1} baseline (not shown in the table; zero learned parameters)
combines six deterministic $[0,1]$ features---tempo match, key match, track-gap
fill, intent-tag overlap, novelty, and recency---with fixed weights that
produce structured evidence codes for every score.
This baseline is always available: when a learned model is unavailable or
times out, the system falls back to \texttt{rules-v1} within two seconds and
labels the result as a fallback.
The user selects an intervention intensity (Nudge: 1, Suggest: 3, Explore: 6
results) to control how many recommendations to receive.

\subsection{Reversible Audiotool Interaction}\label{sec:interaction}

A recommendation can be previewed via protected audio routes and inserted
into the timeline.
Insertion uploads the audio sample to Audiotool, waits for the upload to be
acknowledged, and then modifies the document in a single Nexus transaction.
An \texttt{InsertReceipt} records every created entity ID so that undo
restores the exact prior document state---verified against offline Nexus
documents using the official WASM validator.
Each preview, insertion, and undo event is logged as preference feedback
with the recommendation's rank position, model ID, and request ID.

same-source Recall@10, linear-probe genre accuracy (8 classes), and

\subsection{Baseline Comparison and Multi-Signal Fusion}\label{sec:weak}

We constructed 1,755 leave-one-out pairs from FSLD, using same-pack
membership as a weak proxy for continuation utility, with three negative
types: \emph{easy} (tempo or key incompatible), \emph{hard\_tempo\_key}
(tempo and key both compatible), and \emph{hard\_similar} (highest
embedding cosine from another pack).
Splits are grouped by source pack; all numbers are 5-seed means
(val fraction 0.25).
Table~\ref{tab:weak} reports validation pairwise accuracy.

Frozen cosine similarity (0.883) is already a strong baseline: two
learned rerankers with up to 335K parameters trained with
BPR loss~\cite{rendle2009bpr} score below it (0.735, 0.742),
overfitting to noise in the weak labels.
However, a five-parameter logistic fusion of audio cosine (mean and max
pooling), tempo match, key match, and tag Jaccard overlap---trained with
the same BPR loss---reaches \textbf{0.794 on hard\_similar negatives},
a +4.0\,pp gain over cosine alone (0.754); the gain holds in all 5
seeds (sign test $p = 0.031$).
These are embedding-similar items from different packs where the cosine
signal is uninformative; the gain comes from music-theory compatibility
signals that the embedding cannot encode.
On hard\_tempo\_key negatives, where tempo and key features are neutral
by construction, fusion (0.897) remains close to cosine (0.911),
confirming the improvement is not an artifact of label leakage.

Adding session-structure signals---a zero-shot instrument role-gap
detector and transpose-invariant chroma correlation
(Section~\ref{sec:ranking})---does not improve the fusion: 7-signal
overall (0.892) and hard\_similar (0.792) are indistinguishable from
the 5-signal model.
The \texttt{cosine+session} ablation (0.749 hard\_similar) is slightly
\emph{below} cosine alone, confirming that these compositional signals
are orthogonal to pack-membership labels.
This is expected: pack membership reflects recording context, not
whether a candidate fills a compositional gap; purpose-built evaluation
is needed to assess session-conditioned signals.

\subsection{Author Pilot Evaluation}\label{sec:human}

We preregistered an evaluation protocol (frozen 2026-07-30) before
inspecting any model results.
The primary metric is pairwise accuracy over human continuation
preferences.
As a formative pilot, the author labeled 60 blind A/B pairs with
structured reason codes and a five-point confidence scale;
42 pairs received a preference judgment and 18 were labeled
``neither'' (30\%).
On the 42 preference pairs, \texttt{mean-session-v1} and
\texttt{linear-v1} tie at 0.571 pairwise accuracy
(chance\,=\,0.5); \texttt{rules-v1}, \texttt{recency-v1}, and
\texttt{pocketrank-context-v1} score 0.500;
\texttt{deepsets-v1} falls below chance at 0.405, echoing the
weak-label negative result.

The cosine-agreement rate---how often the CLAP-cosine ranking matches
the human preference---is 57.1\% (24/42), statistically
indistinguishable from chance at this sample size (Wilson 95\% CI
$[0.42, 0.71]$); the pilot cannot yet establish whether human
preference aligns with embedding similarity.
Two descriptive patterns motivate further study: agreement rises
monotonically with annotator confidence (100\% at confidence\,5, 33\%
at confidence\,1), and 30\% of pairs received ``neither''---a judgment
a similarity ranking cannot express.
As an exploratory secondary analysis, the role-gap signal fires on
only 7 of the 42~pairs (most pairs share similar instrumentation),
but agrees with the human preference in 5 of those 7 cases (71.4\%),
suggesting a sparse but precise structural signal.
Author labels are reported separately and excluded from the evaluation
set per the preregistration.

\subsection{External Blind Evaluation}\label{sec:external}

Nine external participants each labeled the same 20~blind A/B pairs
(180~labels total) using a structured form with a five-point confidence
scale.
Table~\ref{tab:external} summarizes the results.
Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.294$ indicates fair inter-rater agreement---typical
for subjective music-preference tasks~\cite{schedl2013music}---with
10 of 20~pairs reaching $\geq$7/9 majority and two pairs achieving
unanimous agreement (9/9).
The overall preference split (A:~51.1\%, B:~40.0\%, Neither:~8.9\%)
shows that most pairs elicit a clear preference, while the low
``neither'' rate (versus the author pilot's 30\%) suggests that context
information aids decision-making.
Mean confidence is $3.07 \pm 1.06$, with higher confidence on
high-agreement pairs.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Formative scope.}
The external evaluation (9~raters, 20~pairs) supplements the
single-annotator pilot with multi-rater data; Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.294$
confirms that continuation preference is subjective but not random.

\textbf{Weak labels $\neq$ ground truth.}
Table~\ref{tab:weak} shows that capacity alone cannot surpass frozen
cosine, but complementary signals (tempo, tags) can---especially on the
hardest negatives.
Weak labels still cannot establish continuation utility; purpose-built
human evaluation remains necessary.

\textbf{Session signals need richer evaluation.}
Role-gap and harmonic signals are implemented and deployed but show no
gain on pack-membership weak labels (Table~\ref{tab:weak}).
This does not mean they are useless for session-conditioned retrieval---it
means pack membership does not exercise the compositional reasoning they
encode.
The author pilot's exploratory analysis (Section~\ref{sec:human}) hints
at role-gap precision (5/7 agreement), but a larger purpose-built study
is needed.
The fingerprint still lacks temporal structure (arrangement position,
transition context) and rhythmic pattern analysis.
Key signature is not available from the Nexus document API.

\textbf{No creativity claims.}
Short-term retrieval experiments cannot support assertions about
enhancing creativity.
We measure perceived relevance and control, not creative output quality.

\textbf{Privacy.}
The system ranks only within a user's own library; audio from sessions is
CLAP-encoded for ranking but never stored by the backend; OAuth tokens
remain in the browser and are never sent to the Pocket Producer server;
feedback export uses an allowlist of named fields with one-way hashed user
IDs.
