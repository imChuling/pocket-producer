\begin{abstract}
Which fragments could grow into a piece of music with the ones
already in a musician's open project?  This target has no direct labels
at cold start, and audio similarity is only one observable
signal for it.  Pocket Producer, a DAW prototype evaluated offline,
treats this missing-label problem as a ranking and an
interaction-design challenge.  It
fingerprints the open session and ranks candidates with a rules
default and a selectable fusion of five interpretable signals
trained on pack co-membership as cold-start weak supervision.  On the
Freesound Loop Dataset, the learned fusion's apparent advantage
varies with label construction, negative regime, and
representation/training configuration.  Pack co-membership is only a stand-in for the real
target, and the conclusions shift with how the proxy is built
and evaluated.  We therefore surface retrieval as
\emph{proposals} the musician can inspect, preview, insert, and
undo, not as automated decisions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ==================================================================

Pocket Producer addresses \textbf{session compatibility}:
whether a library fragment is promising material for the same
piece of music as the fragments already in the open project (the
\emph{session}).
Loop compatibility and sample
retrieval~\cite{chen2020neuralloop,lattner2022samplematch},
stem coherence and
retrieval~\cite{ciranni2025cocola,riou2025stemretrieval},
DAW-integrated and context-conditioned
generation~\cite{he2025tomi,nistal2024diffariff},
and human-in-the-loop co-creation~\cite{elkins2025dawzy} address
parts of this problem.  Unlike pairwise
compatibility~\cite{chen2020neuralloop}, this target is conditioned on the
surrounding session material, as in context-based
retrieval~\cite{lattner2022samplematch,riou2025stemretrieval}.
Audio similarity is one observable signal for this target, not
its definition.  With no direct measurement at cold start, we use
pack co-membership as weak supervision.  Prior work has validated
related proxies against human
judgment~\cite{chen2020neuralloop,lattner2022samplematch}.  Here,
we test how ranking conclusions change with the construction and
evaluation of the proxy.

We contribute: (1)~a framing of DAW fragment retrieval as
ranking for unobserved session compatibility under cold-start
weak supervision;
(2)~a sensitivity analysis across three axes (label construction,
negative regime, and representation/training configuration),
showing how learned fusion's relative performance changes; and
(3)~an offline-evaluated Audiotool prototype that presents
retrieved fragments as inspectable, reversible proposals, with
interaction logging as a path toward direct supervision.

\subsection{Session Fingerprinting and Ranking}\label{sec:ranking}

Pocket Producer (Figure~\ref{fig:system}) reads the Audiotool
session via the Nexus SDK (offline fixtures; live-account
capture pending):
the project tempo, the fragments' tags, and up to eight unique
region-referenced samples embedded with
CLAP~\cite{elizalde2023clap} together form the session
fingerprint.

Offline, five interpretable signals
score loop-only FSLD~\cite{ramires2020fsld} candidates
(deployment: Section~\ref{sec:deployed}) in the context of the
open session:
(i)~mean-pooled and (ii)~max-pooled CLAP cosine similarity to session
samples, (iii)~tempo proximity, (iv)~key match to the session
fragments' FSLD key metadata, and (v)~tag Jaccard overlap with the
session fragments' tags.
Writing $\phi(s,x)\in\mathbb{R}^5$ for these signals given session
$s$ and candidate $x$, the fusion ranks by
$w^\top\phi(s,x)$, with $w$ trained by BPR~\cite{rendle2009bpr}
on pack co-membership weak labels, negatives drawn from a
mixture of regime-specific distributions
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
A hand-tuned \emph{rules} ranker over non-audio signals (tempo,
key, tags, stated intent, recency) remains the default despite
trailing the learned fusion by 16--17\,pp in overall pairwise
ranking accuracy on the held-out proxy; given the proxy's
sensitivity (Section~\ref{sec:observations}), the fusion is
user-selectable rather than default.

\subsection{User-Controlled Proposal Workflow}\label{sec:proposal}

Ranking evidence stays inspectable (per-signal chips) and
creative authority stays with the musician: candidates can be
previewed, inserted via a single Nexus transaction, and undone,
restoring the exact prior entity set (validated offline against
the official Nexus document validator).
Interaction events (preview, accept, reject, insert, undo) are
logged with rank position to support future supervision.

\subsection{Deployment Configuration}\label{sec:deployed}

Deployment adapts the five-signal ranker to the musician's
library.  It uses the MS-CLAP embedding space (licensing, CPU
inference; offline evaluation uses LAION-CLAP Music) and
\emph{source-weighted BPR}: equal weight per source,
hard\_similar negatives $\times$2.0 in the loss, seed-averaged
weights, trained on all train sources.  The key signal is zeroed
online (Audiotool documents lack key metadata), and tag context
comes from the session fragments rather than FSLD pack siblings.

\section{Cold-Start Proxy Evaluation}\label{sec:fusion}
% ==================================================================

\subsection{Weak-Supervision Setup}\label{sec:setup}

With no interaction data at launch, the fusion trains on pack
co-membership as a weak-supervision proxy.
From the FSLD~\cite{ramires2020fsld} we kept only sounds with a
true Freesound pack (1,797 items, 605 packs), discarding
uploader-only grouping; each pack is one \emph{source}, the
grouping unit for all splits.
Each pack with $\geq$3 items yields one leave-one-out query per
item: the left-out item is the positive, while 2--4 random pack
siblings form the session stand-in.  Cross-pack items provide negatives in three
difficulty tiers (\emph{easy}: tempo/key mismatch;
\emph{hard\_tempo\_key}: compatible tempo and key;
\emph{hard\_similar}: highest CLAP cosine to the positive from
another pack).  Two properties of this construction
motivate Section~\ref{sec:observations}: cross-pack negatives can
be false negatives, most plausibly in hard\_similar, and sibling
tags correlate with pack membership, coupling the tag signal to
the proxy label.
We use a fixed 121-source held-out split (seed 20260810);
LAION-CLAP Music~\cite{wu2023laionclap} is the offline backbone.
Development uses the remaining 484 train sources.
We report pairwise ranking accuracy: the fraction of
positive--negative pairs where the positive scores higher (ties
0.5).  Dev-side training uses 5 seeded source-grouped splits.

\subsection{Three-Axis Sensitivity Analysis}\label{sec:observations}

The proxy captures curator-defined pack co-membership, not
session compatibility; conclusions drawn from it shift along
three axes.
%
\textbf{Label construction.}\enspace An earlier corpus treated
same-uploader items as co-members (26\% of examples, 949 sources);
fusion appeared $+4.0$\,pp over frozen cosine on hard\_similar.
Restricting to true pack co-membership erases the dev-set
hard\_similar gain ($+4.0 \to -0.03$\,pp), and fusion trails
cosine on hard\_tempo\_key by 2.1\,pp (negative in all 5 splits); the
earlier advantage may reflect a label-construction
``horse''~\cite{sturm2014horse}.
%
\textbf{Negative regime.}\enspace Leave-one-signal-out ablation
on the dev set shows tempo helps on hard\_similar ($-0.96$\,pp
when removed) but hurts on hard\_tempo\_key ($+0.68$\,pp);
key hurts both dev regimes: removing it adds
$+0.94$/$+1.21$\,pp (five-seed means, no CIs, descriptive).
Held-out under MS-CLAP, tempo's sign pattern recurs but key's
does not ($-0.12$\,pp on hard\_similar, sign unstable across
seeds).  The non-audio rules default falls to 0.424 pairwise
accuracy on hard\_tempo\_key (0.787 overall, held-out, MS-CLAP
column).
%
\textbf{Representation/training configuration.}\enspace All
held-out numbers here, including Table~\ref{tab:heldout} and
Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}, are exploratory and report
corrected runs after a training-protocol mismatch was identified.
Under LAION-CLAP the hard\_similar advantage is
$+2.1$\,pp with a CI crossing zero; under MS-CLAP
with source-weighted BPR (same source split; hard\_similar
re-mined in MS-CLAP space; hard\_similar$\times$2.0
in the loss) it is $+9.9$\,pp.  The two columns differ
in embedding space, training recipe, and mined test negatives,
so the $7.8$\,pp gap illustrates configuration
sensitivity; a four-configuration dev-set factorial shows the
same pattern (Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}b).

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ==================================================================

Pocket Producer frames DAW fragment retrieval as ranking for
unobserved session compatibility under cold-start weak
supervision.  Conclusions about the fusion's advantage over
cosine change across label construction, negative regime, and
representation/training configuration.  Rather than treating proxy scores
as compatibility judgments, the system surfaces retrieval as
inspectable, reversible proposals.
Future work will replace the cold-start proxy with
interaction-derived evidence and evaluate retrieval against
musicians' creative choices.
