\begin{abstract}
We present Pocket Producer, a prototype that operationalizes
\emph{session-conditioned continuation retrieval} inside the Audiotool
DAW\@.  A five-signal logistic fusion, trained with BPR on pack
co-membership weak labels from the Freesound Loop Dataset, serves as
the cold-start candidate generator; a production variant of the same
recipe is deployed in the system.
Four lines of evidence show this proxy should not decide
autonomously:
(1)~the apparent fusion gain is label-sensitive---+4.0\,pp over
frozen cosine on hard\_similar under mixed uploader/pack labels, but
$-0.03$\,pp under pack-only labels;
(2)~leave-one-signal-out ablation shows regime-dependent
contributions---tempo helps on hard\_similar but hurts on
hard\_tempo\_key;
(3)~on a pre-committed held-out split the fusion$-$cosine difference
is small and uncertain (hard\_similar $+1.5$\,pp, CI crossing zero)
and significantly negative on hard\_tempo\_key;
(4)~an exploratory pilot shows a descriptive divergence between
weak-label signal rankings and human consensus.
Pocket Producer therefore treats every recommendation as a
\emph{proposal}: candidates are previewable, insertion is reversible,
and feedback is logged for future personalization (not yet
evaluated).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ==================================================================

When a musician returns to an unfinished loop-based project, the
question is not ``what sounds similar?''\ but ``what would be useful
as the next step?''
Loop compatibility~\cite{chen2020neuralloop,lattner2022samplematch,yi2024drum2bass,roychaudhuri2026loopmatcher},
stem coherence~\cite{ciranni2025cocola,nistal2024diffariff,riou2025stemretrieval},
session-conditioned generation~\cite{he2025tomi}, and reversible
co-creation~\cite{elkins2025dawzy} all address parts of this problem.
We define \textbf{continuation utility} as the property that a
candidate helps the musician make progress in the current session;
unlike similarity it is asymmetric and context-dependent.

We contribute: (1)~a DAW-integrated prototype that deploys the
evaluated five-signal fusion as a selectable ranker and treats
retrieval as user-controlled proposal, not automatic decision;
(2)~empirical evidence that weak-label proxy scores are sensitive to
label construction, regime-dependent, uncertain on held-out data,
and possibly misaligned with human judgment---motivating keeping
creative authority with the musician.

\section{System}\label{sec:system}
% ==================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{assets/system-figure}}
  \caption{Pocket Producer pipeline: the system proposes
  (steps~1--3); the musician decides (steps~4--6).}
  \label{fig:system}
\end{figure}

The live Audiotool session (Figure~\ref{fig:system}) is read via
the Nexus SDK and mapped to a session fingerprint (tempo, and CLAP
embeddings~\cite{elizalde2023clap} of up to eight unique
region-referenced samples).
A five-signal logistic fusion trained with BPR
loss~\cite{rendle2009bpr} ranks candidates across timbral similarity
(CLAP cosine to session regions, mean- and max-pooled), tempo match,
key compatibility, and tag overlap.
The deployed ranker follows the same five-signal recipe but is a
separate production model, with four stated deltas from the offline
evaluation: it runs in the production MS-CLAP space (offline:
LAION-CLAP Music), with weights retrained there on the pack-only
train side (mean over 5 seeds); the key term is constant zero online
(Audiotool documents carry no key signature); and tag context comes
from fragments used in the session, not pack siblings.
Given the results below, the \emph{default} ranker is the
transparent rules baseline; fusion remains selectable and falls back
to rules without session audio.
Candidates are surfaced for preview---with per-signal evidence
(audio similarity, tempo fit, tag overlap)---before any session
state changes.
Accepted candidates are inserted in a single Nexus transaction with
undo; rejected or undone events are logged.
This design responds to Section~\ref{sec:evidence}'s findings: the
proxy must not substitute its ranking for the musician's judgment.

\section{Weak-Label Fusion}\label{sec:fusion}
% ==================================================================

From the FSLD~\cite{ramires2020fsld} we kept only sounds with a
true Freesound pack (1,797 items, 605 packs), discarding items
grouped only by uploader.
Each eligible pack with at least three items yields one leave-one-out query per item, with
same-pack siblings as positives and cross-pack items as negatives in
three difficulty tiers (\emph{easy}: tempo/key mismatch;
\emph{hard\_tempo\_key}: compatible tempo and key;
\emph{hard\_similar}: highest cosine to the positive from another
pack).
A 121-source held-out split (seed 20260810) was committed to version
control together with the evaluation protocol \emph{before} any
evaluation ran.
Development uses only the 484 train sources; the 171 with $\geq$3
items form queries (981 examples), the rest contribute negatives
only.
LAION-CLAP Music~\cite{wu2023laionclap} (512-dim) provides the
embedding backbone.

On the train side (mean over 5 source-grouped splits), frozen cosine
reaches 0.929 overall and 0.814 on hard\_similar; five-signal fusion
reaches 0.925 and 0.813.
Under pack-only labels the fusion advantage is gone: $-0.03$\,pp on
hard\_similar, and fusion trails cosine on hard\_tempo\_key by
2.1\,pp on average (negative in all 5 splits).
These development-set numbers are descriptive; inference is reserved
for the held-out evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:heldout}).

\section{Why the Proxy Should Not Decide Alone}%
\label{sec:evidence}
% ==================================================================

Four lines of evidence jointly motivate treating the proxy as a
proposal rather than a decision.

\subsection{The Gain Is Label-Sensitive}\label{sec:labels}

An earlier version used the manifest's fallback grouping, treating
\emph{same-uploader} items as co-members in 26\% of examples.
On that mixed-label corpus, fusion appeared to beat frozen cosine by
+4.0\,pp on hard\_similar (949 sources, same protocol).
Restricting to true pack co-membership erases the gain
(Section~\ref{sec:fusion}).
The restriction changes the source universe and mined negatives
together, so the loss cannot be attributed to the uploader-grouped
examples alone; what it shows is that the measured advantage is
sensitive to label construction---too sensitive to treat as a
reliable utility estimate.

Table~\ref{tab:loso} shows leave-one-signal-out deltas per negative
regime.  Removing tempo costs $-0.96$\,pp on hard\_similar but
\emph{gains} $+0.68$\,pp on hard\_tempo\_key; removing key helps on
both hard regimes ($+0.94$\,/\,$+1.21$\,pp).
Both patterns replicate directionally on the held-out split (tempo
$-1.48$\,/\,$+0.37$\,pp, key $+0.62$\,/\,$+0.97$\,pp).
Part of this pattern is built in: easy and hard\_tempo\_key are
constructed from tempo and key, so those signals are near-constant
within them by design.
Still, a fused score averages over regimes where signals switch
sign, so its ranking cannot be uniformly trusted.

\subsection{Held-Out Evaluation}\label{sec:heldout}

The held-out split was evaluated exactly once, after all
development; protocol and split were committed first, making the
freeze verifiable in repository history.
Of its 121 sources, 54 form queries (323 examples); fusion was
retrained on the train side with the same hyperparameters and 5
seeds.
The fusion$-$cosine difference on hard\_similar is $+1.5$\,pp with a
source-grouped bootstrap 95\%~CI of $[-1.5, +4.7]$\,pp (joint
resampling of the 54 example-bearing source clusters, shared across
seeds)---small and uncertain.
On hard\_tempo\_key the CI is entirely below zero in this secondary
analysis ($-1.0$\,pp); overall the difference
is $+0.4$\,pp with a CI crossing zero.

Residual limitations: the signal set and hyperparameters come from
the earlier mixed-label corpus, which subsumes this one; the run
evaluates the offline LAION-CLAP model, not the deployed MS-CLAP
variant, and is a sensitivity re-analysis, not de novo confirmation.

\subsection{Exploratory Pilot}\label{sec:pilot}

Nine external raters labeled 20 blind A/B pairs (Fleiss'
$\kappa = 0.294$).
One pair whose majority chose ``neither'' was excluded; 9 of the
remaining 19 preference pairs reached $\geq$7/9 consensus.
On these 9 pairs, harmonic compatibility---whose weak-label
contribution was near zero in the earlier mixed-label 7-signal
ablation---matches the human majority in 7/9 cases, while
tempo, the strongest hard\_similar contributor in
Table~\ref{tab:loso}, matches in 3/9---a descriptive divergence
from the weak-label ranking.
The comparison is exploratory---post-hoc, small, and on a different
corpus and pair construction---raising the possibility of
misalignment with human judgment without confirming it.

% ==================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ==================================================================

Pocket Producer operationalizes session-conditioned retrieval in a
DAW\@.
The weak-label fusion is an \emph{operational} cold-start candidate
generator---comparable to frozen cosine on held-out data ($+0.4$\,pp
overall, $+1.5$\,pp hard\_similar, both CIs crossing zero; worse on
hard\_tempo\_key)---that, unlike a bare cosine score, surfaces
per-signal evidence with each proposal.
Since its contributions are regime-dependent and may not reflect
human preference, it never decides alone: every recommendation is
previewable and reversible.
Whether preview and undo improve creative outcomes is untested;
evaluating them, like personalization from logged feedback, is
future work.
