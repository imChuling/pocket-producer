\begin{abstract}
Musicians accumulate large libraries of unfinished audio fragments yet
get little support deciding what to try next when re-entering a
project.
We formulate \emph{session-conditioned continuation retrieval}: given the
live state of a DAW project, rank a musician's own fragments by their
utility for the next creative step.
Pocket Producer implements this in Audiotool: a transparent fingerprint
of the live project, ranking with frozen audio--text representations,
and reversible single-transaction insertion.
On 1,755 weak pairs from the Freesound Loop Dataset, frozen CLAP cosine
reaches 0.883 pairwise accuracy and learned rerankers (up to 335K
parameters) cannot beat it; a five-parameter fusion of cosine, tempo,
key, and tag signals reaches 0.794 on the hardest negatives, +4.0\,pp
over cosine (0.754; one-sided sign test $p = 0.031$).
Session-structure signals (instrument role gap, harmonic compatibility)
show no gain on pack-membership labels, while an exploratory alignment
of the same signals with external rater preferences (9~raters,
20~pairs, Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.294$) points the opposite
way---weak-label and human evaluations appear to reward different
structure, motivating task-specific evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

When a musician returns to an unfinished loop-based project, the central
question is not ``what sounds similar?''\ but ``what would be useful as
the next step?''
Existing work on loop compatibility~\cite{chen2020neuralloop,lattner2022samplematch,yi2024drum2bass}
and stem coherence~\cite{ciranni2024cocola,nistal2024diffariff}
asks whether two elements can co-exist in a mix; our query is a
\emph{live session state}, and the candidates are the musician's own
creative memory, not a public catalog.

We define \textbf{continuation utility} as the property that a candidate
fragment helps the musician make progress in the current
session---subsuming tempo fit, timbral contrast, structural gap-filling,
and stated intent.
We contribute:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Task:} session-conditioned ranking of personal fragments
  with a preregistered primary metric (pairwise accuracy over human
  preferences).
\item \textbf{System:} a working prototype in a real DAW (Audiotool)
  with live document reading, reversible insertion, and explainable
  evidence.
\item \textbf{Protocol:} a backbone bake-off, a baseline comparison
  (learned rerankers lose to frozen cosine; multi-signal fusion gains
  +4.0\,pp on hard negatives), and a blind A/B evaluation with
  9~external raters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Session Fingerprint}\label{sec:fingerprint}

The live Nexus document is mapped deterministically to a
\texttt{SessionFingerprint} (tempo, track counts and types, playhead,
user-editable intent); only this summary leaves the browser, and
missing fields are never imputed.
Session audio regions are encoded with
MS-CLAP 2023~\cite{elizalde2023clap} into a mean session embedding
(CLAP's joint audio--text space also serves text intent); per-region
instrument roles and 12-dim CQT chroma are extracted for the
session-structure signals below.

\subsection{Continuation Utility Ranking}\label{sec:ranking}

Candidate fragments are scored in two stages.
First, per-space retrieval runs independently over four signal spaces:
(1)~structured metadata (tempo/key/tag matching),
(2)~frozen CLAP embeddings (cosine similarity),
(3)~\emph{role-gap fill}---a zero-shot instrument probe boosts candidates
whose predicted role fills a gap in the session, and
(4)~\emph{harmonic compatibility}---transpose-invariant cross-correlation
of CQT chroma vectors scores tonal fit across all 12 semitone shifts.
Results are fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion~\cite{cormack2009rrf};
a learned reranker can be applied on top, and a rule baseline with
structured evidence codes is the always-available fallback.

\subsection{Reversible Audiotool Interaction}\label{sec:interaction}

A recommendation can be previewed and inserted in a single Nexus
transaction; an \texttt{InsertReceipt} records created entity IDs so
undo restores the exact prior state (verified with the official WASM
validator); interactions are logged as preference feedback.

\subsection{Baseline Comparison and Multi-Signal Fusion}\label{sec:weak}

We constructed 1,755 leave-one-out pairs from FSLD, using same-pack
membership as a weak proxy for continuation utility, with three negative
types: \emph{easy} (tempo or key incompatible), \emph{hard\_tempo\_key}
(tempo and key both compatible), and \emph{hard\_similar} (highest
embedding cosine from another pack).
Splits are grouped by source pack; Table~\ref{tab:weak} reports 5-seed
mean validation pairwise accuracy.

Frozen cosine similarity (0.883) is already a strong baseline: two
learned rerankers with up to 335K parameters trained with
BPR loss~\cite{rendle2009bpr} score below it (0.735, 0.742),
overfitting to noise in the weak labels.
However, a five-parameter logistic fusion of audio cosine (mean and max
pooling), tempo match, key match, and tag Jaccard overlap---trained with
the same BPR loss---reaches \textbf{0.794 on hard\_similar negatives},
a +4.0\,pp gain over cosine alone (0.754); the gain holds in all 5
seeds (one-sided sign test $p = 0.031$).
Hard\_similar negatives are by construction cosine's failure mode
(mined as highest-cosine from another pack): the +4.0\,pp measures
what complementary signals recover there---the overall gain is
+1.1\,pp, holding in all 5 paired splits.
On hard\_tempo\_key negatives, where tempo and key features are neutral
by construction, fusion (0.897) stays close to cosine (0.911), ruling
out label leakage.

Adding the session-structure signals (Section~\ref{sec:ranking}) does
not improve the fusion: 7-signal results (0.892 overall, 0.792
hard\_similar) are indistinguishable from 5-signal, and
\texttt{cosine+session} (0.749) sits slightly below cosine
alone---pack membership reflects recording context, not whether a
candidate fills a compositional gap.

\subsection{Author Pilot Evaluation}\label{sec:human}

Under a preregistered protocol (frozen 2026-07-30), the author labeled
60 blind A/B pairs; 42 received a preference, 18 ``neither'' (30\%).
No ranker exceeds 0.571 pairwise accuracy (chance\,=\,0.5), and
cosine-agreement is 57.1\% (24/42)---statistically indistinguishable
from chance (Wilson 95\% CI $[0.42, 0.71]$).
The role-gap signal fires on only 7 of 42~pairs but agrees in 5 of 7.
Author labels are excluded per the preregistration.

\subsection{External Blind Evaluation}\label{sec:external}

Nine external raters labeled the same 20~blind A/B pairs
(180~labels).
Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.294$ indicates fair inter-rater
agreement---typical for subjective music-preference
tasks~\cite{schedl2013music}---with 10 of 20~pairs reaching
$\geq$7/9 majority.
Most pairs elicit a clear preference (only 8.9\% ``neither'' versus
the pilot's 30\%), suggesting context information aids
decision-making.

In a post-hoc exploratory analysis (not preregistered; five signals
compared), we align each signal with the per-pair human majority.
On rater-consensus pairs ($\geq$7/9), harmonic compatibility matches
the majority in 7 of 9 cases, CLAP cosine in 6, max-pooled cosine in
4, and tempo---the strongest theory signal on weak labels---in 3;
the sparse role-gap signal is correct on all 3~pairs where it fires,
echoing the pilot's 5/7.
No single difference is significant at $n = 9$, but the direction is
consistent: the signals that win on weak labels
(Table~\ref{tab:weak}) lose ground on human preferences and vice
versa, suggesting the two evaluations reward different structure and
motivating a preregistered replication.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Weak labels $\neq$ ground truth.}
Weak labels compare signals at scale but cannot establish continuation
utility---and can invert the human signal ranking
(Section~\ref{sec:external}).
Offline numbers use LAION-CLAP; fusion on deployed MS-CLAP is
untested.

\textbf{Scope and privacy.}
We measure perceived relevance, not creative quality; session audio is
never stored and OAuth tokens never leave the browser.
